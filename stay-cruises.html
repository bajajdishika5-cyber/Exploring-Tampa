Stay & Cruises

Where to stay

Pick your base

Stay downtown for the Riverwalk, in Ybor for nightlife and history, or on the sand across the bay. Sailing from Port Tampa Bay? Downtown and Channel District hotels put you minutes from the cruise terminals — see the Cruises section.

Area

Downtown & Riverwalk

Luxury high-rise hotels and the JW Marriott / Marriott Water Street pair, steps from the aquarium, arena and streetcar.

Area

Ybor City & Hyde Park

Boutique stays like Hotel Haya in Ybor and the Epicurean in Hyde Park — character, dining and walkable streets.

Area

Davis Islands

A quiet, leafy island neighborhood minutes from downtown, with marinas and Bayshore Boulevard at its edge.

Area · Beachfront

Clearwater Beach & St. Pete Beach

Beachfront resorts and family-friendly condos where the Gulf is the backyard; mid-range and budget motels line Gulf Boulevard.

By budget & style

Every kind of stay

Luxury resorts, mid-range chains near Westshore and the airport, budget motels along Dale Mabry, family suites near Busch Gardens, and boutique inns in Hyde Park Village.

Rentals & outdoors

Rentals, RV parks & camping

Vacation rentals in Seminole Heights and on the beaches, RV resorts around the bay, and tent camping at Fort De Soto and Hillsborough River State Park.

Cruises

Sail away from Port Tampa Bay

For many visitors, the ship is the reason for the trip. Port Tampa Bay, right downtown in the Channel District, is one of the busiest cruise homeports in the United States — and unlike most ports, it sits in the middle of the city, so sailing day comes with a city worth arriving early for.

Channel District · DowntownHomeport

Port Tampa Bay

The cruise terminals line Channelside Drive beside The Florida Aquarium and Sparkman Wharf, minutes from downtown hotels and the fare-free TECO Streetcar. Ships sail out through the bay and under the Sunshine Skyway Bridge to begin the trip. Check in, drop your bags, and you can still be back on the Riverwalk for lunch.

Port Tampa Bay cruise guide ↗

Who sails from Tampa — 2026–27 season

Year-round + seasonal

Carnival Cruise Line

Carnival Paradise sails from Tampa all year on 4- to 6-night trips, and Carnival Legend joins for the winter season with 6- and 7-night sailings. Both board at Terminal 3.

Year-round + seasonal

Royal Caribbean

Radiance of the Seas and Enchantment of the Seas sail year-round, with Jewel of the Seas adding winter-season departures — 4- to 8-night Western Caribbean and Bahamas trips from Terminals 2 and 3.

Year-round

Margaritaville at Sea

The Islander sails year-round from Terminal 6, mostly on relaxed 4- and 5-night runs to Key West and Mexico.

Winter season

Norwegian Cruise Line

Norwegian Gem homeports in Tampa from about November to April, with everything from 4-night getaways to longer 10- to 12-night Caribbean voyages, usually from Terminal 6.

Winter season

Celebrity Cruises

Celebrity Summit spends the winter season in Tampa, sailing 7-night trips that mix the Bahamas with Mexico.

Good to know

Before you book

Ships, seasons and terminals change. The line-up above is the 2026–27 season, checked against the port's published cruise calendar in November 2026. Always confirm your ship, terminal and check-in time in your own cruise documents.

Where the ships go

Most popular

Western Caribbean

The classic Tampa route: Cozumel and Costa Maya in Mexico, Roatán in Honduras, Belize City and Grand Cayman — reefs, Mayan ruins and beach days on 5- to 7-night sailings.

Short trips

Mexico getaways

Easy 4- and 5-night trips to Cozumel, Costa Maya and Progreso on the Yucatán coast — a great first cruise, with a sea day to enjoy the ship.

Select sailings

Bahamas & Key West

Nassau and Bimini, cruise-line private islands, and stops in Key West for the sunset at Mallory Square — look for these on select 4- to 7-night sailings.

Terminals — check your documents

  • Terminal 2: 651 Channelside Drive, beside The Florida Aquarium.
  • Terminal 3: 815 Channelside Drive, the central terminal, across from the parking garage.
  • Terminal 6: 1333 McKay Street, with its own lot next door.
  • Your cruise line assigns your terminal — it is printed on your boarding documents, so check before you set the GPS.

Parking & getting there

  • Official parking: the Channelside Parking Garage (810 Channelside Drive) serves Terminals 2 and 3; the McKay Street lot serves Terminal 6.
  • The port's tariff lists self-parking at Parking is $19 a day (the port's rate since October 1, 2026), with valet as a $20 upgrade — prepay on the port's website and confirm the current rate before you go.
  • From the airport: Tampa International (TPA) is roughly 20 minutes away by car, taxi or rideshare.
  • The fare-free TECO Streetcar stops in the Channel District, a short walk from Terminals 2 and 3.

Arrive a day early

  • Flights get delayed; ships don't wait. Fly or drive in the day before and stay downtown, in the Channel District or in Ybor City — see Where to Stay.
  • Spend the extra day like a local: the aquarium by the port in the morning, lunch at Sparkman Wharf, the streetcar to Ybor City, and the Riverwalk at sunset.
  • Staying a night after your cruise works too — add a Gulf beach day before you head home.

A pre-cruise day, Tampa style

  1. Morning: Check in to a downtown or Channel District hotel, then walk to The Florida Aquarium — it is right by the terminals.
  2. Midday: Lunch at Sparkman Wharf and a stroll along the Riverwalk, then ride the fare-free streetcar to Ybor City for the visitor center and 7th Avenue.
  3. Evening: Dinner in Ybor City or downtown, boarding documents and bags ready, and an early night — embarkation day goes smoother rested.
  4. Sailing day: Head to your assigned terminal for your cruise line's check-in time; official parking is across the street or right beside the terminal.
